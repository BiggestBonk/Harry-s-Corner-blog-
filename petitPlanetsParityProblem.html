Petit Planet's Parity Problem

With the release date for Hoyoverse's latest game, Petit Planet finally confirmed for December 4th, many players will be pleased to know that they'll have the game in their hands before year end. The one caveat to this news though, is that the game only releases on PC and mobile on that date. A Switch 2 version has already been confirmed for 2027, but with all the buzz that will undoubtedly surround the game after its global launch, I can't help but feel like skipping a simultaneous release on PS5 and Xbox Series consoles is a huge missed opportunity.

“Do You Guys Not Have Phones”?

When I look at the way Petit Planet's launch is scheduled, I can't help but feel like a lot of players on console platforms (specifically PS5 and Xbox Series) will see the hype around the game's launch and rush to their console of choice's storefront, only to be sorely disappointed when they find the game doesn't even have a listing yet.

The rather obvious rebuttal to this problem is: "You have a phone, don't you? Just play it on there", and this isn't a meritless argument. Of course almost every console player will have access to a smartphone capable of playing the game on release to some capacity. I'd argue that it isn't the silver bullet it sounds like on its face though, as plenty of console players don't use their phones as a gaming device often, if at all.
Realistically speaking, most phones aren't particularly great devices for gaming on, unless the game in question is tailored specifically for them. For most console players (including myself), if a multiplatform game like those developed by Hoyoverse or Kuro Games releases, I'm going to try to play it on the hardware that will have the easiest time running it, not the most "convenient" option, as my outdated phone would probably overheat, and the controls would be fiddly at the best of times.

Ignoring The Hungriest Mouths

As someone who's primarily used a PS5 without access to a decent PC for a long time, when faced with a scenario like this I'd just ignore the game entirely until it releases on PS5. This would work out fine for myself in this scenario, but not so much for Hoyoverse's hopes of gaining boatloads of players at launch.

I honestly think that this issue is made doubly worse in Petit Planet's case by the fact that Xbox Series and Playstation consoles have access to comparatively few cozy village management games compared to PC and the Nintendo Switch family of consoles. Missing out on the chance to play a free-to-play iteration of a genre that many Playstation and Xbox players haven't played much of (if at all) at launch feels like a surefire way for Hoyoverse to miss out on some of their most keen and excitable potential players.

Too Little (and Too much), Too Late

The other thing about this launch strategy that I think could really come back to bite Hoyoverse in the long run, is that whenever the game does eventually drop on all consoles (as it almost inevitably will), players who've decided to wait will feel a creeping sense of FOMO.
Excited players will jump into the game for the first time and get through the early tutorialising, to then be inundated with a year's worth of added content, and find that they've missed events and gacha banners that they would've liked to have had a shot at (or even spent money on). This will leave those players feeling shortchanged, and Hoyoverse with less of their money, making it a lose-lose scenario. I don't think that this will put off many players entirely, but I do think that those on the PS5 and Xbox Series X/S will feel snubbed compared to those who've already been enjoying the game for the past year (or however long the port takes to release).

Ultimately, I think that Hoyoverse would've been much better served by going for a simultaneous multiplatform release, despite the extra time it would've taken. Now that a concrete release date has been confirmed for PC and Mobile though, there's no feasible way to put the genie back in the bottle. They've made their decision, and I honestly still believe the game will be a hit, but I can't help but feel like it won't have the impact or reach it otherwise could have due to their release strategy.

Anecdotally, I can't help but think back to moments like the console release of Fortnite's Battle Royale mode, and just how huge it was due to Playstation players not having access to similar games or modes (i.e. Arma 3 Battle Royale or PUBG) yet. I think something very similar could've happened here with Petit Planet, but now we won't get to know.
