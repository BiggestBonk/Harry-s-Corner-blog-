A Bit More About Me

Who am I? What are my career goals?

I'm a software tester based in New Zealand with a background in software development. After completing my software development studies, I realised that my strong attention to detail and analytical mindset made me a stronger fit for testing rather than development, which is something I'd already been doing informally for years through beta testing video games. Since then, I've been fully committed to building my QA career through hands-on projects, volunteer work, and freelance testing work, while developing my automation skills through using tools such as Playwright and Selenium.

What work would I be interested in doing?

I'm particularly drawn to the investigative side of testing, and finding the edge cases or overlooked vulnerabilities that fall by the wayside when teams are focused on trying to get a product to market. I find real satisfaction in being the person who asks 'but what happens if...?' and uncovering potential issues before they reach users. I also have a broad knowledge base across various fields, which makes me well suited to cross-functional teams where understanding the bigger picture and communicating across different levels is just as important as the testing itself.

What's something I am paricularly passionate about?

I'm deeply passionate about accessibility and the idea that everything, not just software, should be usable by as wide a range of people as possible. I believe the internet and the tools we build should be intuitive and inclusive by default, not as an afterthought. This passion feeds directly into my testing approach, as I'm not just looking for bugs, I'm testing to see whether the experience works consistently for everyone who might encounter it, not just the ideal user in the ideal scenario.

What are my unique qualities and abilities?

One of my strongest qualities is that I think about situations holistically. When I encounter any system, whether it's a piece software, a game, or an organisation, I instinctively want to understand the whole picture: I want to know how it was built, who it serves, how each part interacts, and where the seams are. This isn't something I've had to develop professionally, it's just how my mind likes to categorise information. When gaming, I find myself analysing character/build synergies and counter-play, rather than just focussing on my personal performance. In terms of testing software, I'm drawn to understanding how the product was built, and who uses it before I start writing a test plan. This makes me effective at finding the kinds of issues that only become visible when you understand the full context of the product, rather than focussing purely on an individual feature.
