NZ & American Idol Politics

Paying attention to this year's election coverage in New Zealand has been a painful process. It's been tiresome to see TVNZ and The NZ Herald's clear favouritism towards the major political parties (Labour and National respectively), and the absurd popularity contests or “polls” that each of them have held. After hearing the hosts of these debates incessantly beg the audience to pick the candidate they felt was performing the best multiple times during the debate, I couldn't help but be reminded of the ridiculous text lines early 2000s talent contest shows like 'American Idol' would open up to the public as a thinly veiled attempt to drive viewer engagement.

The thing that makes these “polls” feel totally vestigial to myself (and most people I know), is that almost no one is choosing their preferred party in polls like these based on who they thought performed particularly well or "won" the specific debate. I highly doubt that anyone who actually tuned in to watch any of the minor party or leaders debates, actually walked away thinking that a party that they aren't politically aligned with performed particularly well, let alone well enough to throw a vote their way.

Take the minor party debate that took place on TVNZ on Tuesday [1] as an example: It felt quite clear to me that if you were going off the metrics of what constitutes a proper and fair debate (e.g. not constantly interrupting your opponent's talking points, etc.), the leader of Te Pati Maori, Rawiri Waititi, conducted himself in the most reasonable and measured fashion out of all the leaders present that night by a long shot. Yet when the poll results were finalised from TVNZ's “Vote Compass”, absolutely no one was surprised to see that the majority conservative viewership and voter base firmly backed Act's David Seymour as their appointed debate champion. Meanwhile, Waititi didn't even move the needle enough to register.

At least from what I can gather, this result wasn't due to Seymour holding himself to a high standard, or because he made particularly intelligent points during this debate. Instead, it was due to the overwhelming majority of people who tuned into this debate (and many similar such debates) already having their minds made up from the second it began. In the current tribal political climate of New Zealand, no one wants to be caught betraying their side. Ultimately, the poll's question could've easily been changed to "Which party's biggest fans are the most active on TVNZ's 'Vote Compass'?", and the results would've remained the same.

For someone like myself, I frankly didn't see any value in participating in this silly little exercise, and I'm sure the sentiment is much the same for anyone else who isn't already deeply entrenched in the reactionary rage bait politics offered by New Zealand's mass media. Outside of the context of these publication's respective ecosystems, the votes submitted to their polls mean absolutely nothing. They simply serve as an opportunity to give your party of choice a little pat on the back, and give TVNZ or The Herald a few cents from your internet traffic. Unfortunately, these types of televised debates rarely sway anyone on the discussed issues, and people just vote for the party they've assigned themselves to already.

In conclusion, I'm going to be saving my energy for literally anything else, and making sure to vote when my choice (and the results) actually matter. I'd advise anyone reading this to do the same, and totally ignore any polling (or other methods of outreach) from any of these biased and cynical businesses that fatten their wallets by masquerading as outlets of fair and objective journalism.
